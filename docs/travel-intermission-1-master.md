# First Travel Intermission — Loftwick to Hardby — Master GM Walkthrough

*A spoon-fed run guide. Read top to bottom; the order on this page is the order at the table.*

---

## What this intermission is for

The Commercial Cover road out of Loftwick is **six weeks long**, and until now the module crossed it in a paragraph. Six weeks of nothing teaches the table that the choice they made in *Scene 7 · Three Roads East* was a menu item. Six weeks with three fights in it teaches them it was a decision.

Three encounters, each doing different work, in escalating order of what is at stake:

1. **The Fallow Ford** (Day 6, land). A monster that does not care who they are. What it tests is the **cover**, which is the only asset the conspiracy cannot buy. How loudly the party wins is recorded on a tally and paid back in the second encounter.
2. **The Grey Fee** (Day 29, boarding). The plot encounter. A Wild Coast freebooter comes alongside for cargo and turns out to be holding a circular with the party's number on it. Ends with a letter signed **E.**
3. **The Grey-Back** (Day 37, sea). The consequence encounter. The dead from the boarding went over the side and something followed the hull for a week. What is at stake is the ship and the crew — which makes it the only one of the three the party can lose while surviving it.

Party tier: written for **five PCs at 5th level** (PC-01, Cam absent). This is also where the party makes 6th, which is where Phase 3 expects them (levels 6–7).

## The three canon anchors this must not break

1. **The firm suspects; it does not know.** *GM Brief — Phase 3 Purpose* fixes the Commercial Cover head start at about five weeks of alarm with no confirmation the party is in Hardby. The circular is a **guess**, sent to four masters, and says so in its own text. Rewrite it into certainty and you have rewritten Phase 3's opening position.
2. **"E." stays a single initial.** *The Veska Reveal — Reading the Chops* is where the fourth hand gets named as a hand, and Veska's gift is not the letter but the **three details of how that hand forms it** — the loop, the serif, the angle of the descender. So the circular is a **clerk's fair copy**: the initial survives the transcription, the hand does not. When Veska gives the three details, the party comes back to this sheet and finds it does not match, which is a finding of its own — somebody in a Hardby office copied out orders from a hand nobody in Hardby admits to having seen. Her scene gets stronger; nothing is spent.
3. **The courier's mark is the party's own recognition.** The wax carries the mark from *Seed Repairs — What Phase 2 Was Missing*, in its exact words: *a single upright stroke with a short bar across it, up near the top*. They found it behind a loose stone on Tannery Row and again on Merev Sarth's forged writ. No NPC reads it for them.

## The six weeks

Canon, from *Arrival — Commercial Cover*: six weeks, two of them the ride down the Sheldomar and the wait for a hull, the rest aboard the **Kestrel Wife**, whose master *put in twice at ports nobody told you about, because he is a trader before he is anybody's courier*. Hardby comes up at **dusk on the fortieth day**. Those two unannounced ports are already in the shipped text, and they are the mechanism by which the boarding happens — which means the party's bad luck is nobody's invention.

| Day | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Wool Road, Loftwick to Stell | Ground they have seen if they ran the Brown Hare. |
| **6** | Sheldomar bottom-land | **Encounter 1 — The Fallow Ford.** |
| 7–11 | Into Keoland proper | Tolls, wool-houses. Gianni's map thread can fire. |
| 12–14 | Gradsul | The berth, Roke's terms, **the fire rule said out loud**. |
| 20–22 | First unannounced port | Where the hull's name gets loose. |
| **29** | Pomarj shore, Highport reach | **Encounter 2 — The Grey Fee.** |
| 31–35 | Rounding into Woolly Bay | Repairs, a burial, a crew who keep mentioning grey-backs. |
| **37** | Inside Woolly Bay | **Encounter 3 — The Grey-Back.** |
| **40** | Hardby, dusk | Hand off to *Arrival — Commercial Cover*, two lines adjusted. |

## Encounter 1 — The Fallow Ford (Day 6)

**Six ankhegs, one of them old. 2,700 XP.** *(Six PCs: a seventh, +450.)*

The party travels with **Hennet Vole**, a real Keoish drover with four carts of fleeces bound for the Gradsul market — eleven oxen, three drovers, a boy. That is the whole reason he exists: **there are witnesses, and the witnesses are going to the same market where the party will shortly choose a hull.**

- The oxen refuse the ford. **Survival DC 12** (free to Gianni and Kitty) reads it as ground-sense. Give them one round before the first ankheg comes up under the second cart.
- The brood hunts the **oxen**, not the party. Every grapple drags toward a tunnel mouth, so every grapple is a countdown rather than a damage roll — which is what makes it dangerous to a party with no front line.
- The old one does not close. It surfaces at range and sprays the **sample cart** in preference to a person.
- **Terrain, three pieces:** the ditch (cover, and acid does not turn corners), the ford (difficult ground; ankhegs will not cross moving water), the carts (the point).
- **Cart clock, three rounds.** A spray on the bales, or two ankhegs grappling one cart at the start of a round, ruins the wool samples: three days and ~140 gp in Gradsul, and letters of introduction that no longer match the cargo.
- **Ankheg sheets come from your monster compendium** — this module ships no beasts (155 of its 157 NPCs are humanoid, the other two constructs). Use your compendium's numbers; give the old one +20 hp and a 5–6 recharge.

**The visibility tally.** Count loud acts: any visible-area spell, any casting a drover can describe afterwards, a wild shape in the open. **0–1 quiet / 2–3 mixed / 4+ or any fireball loud.** It sets one line of the circular and nothing else, and the party never learns the rule existed.

## Encounter 2 — The Grey Fee (Day 29)

**On the party's side of the deck: 2,850 XP** — Mattick Sarn (CR 4), Loy Tarrow (CR 3), Vennick Ord (CR 2), two topmen and four boarders (CR 1/2). **Second wave, the dial: four more boarders, +400.** *(Six PCs: two more in the first wave, +200.)*

- **Two rounds before initiative.** Perception DC 13 (her deck is too clean), DC 16 or any sailor (men lying behind the rail), Insight DC 14 on the man in the good coat — Ord in *disguise self*, touching the cuff. Let the setup matter: fouling the irons or breaking the plank buys a round.
- **Do not put the other fourteen pirates on the map.** The Kestrel Wife's eleven hands fight for their own hull: one d20 a round, 11+ holds, 6–10 gives a step at −2, 5 or less breaks and releases the second wave. A PC who spends an action on the crew line gives the next roll advantage.
- **Three clocks.** The berth (3 rounds — two boarders go for the strongbox, which holds **the decoy**; the real package is sewn into a bale). The slow-match (Tarrow at the fore hatch: 4 rounds, then a six-segment burn clock — **and the party's own fire rolls it**). The count (round 2–3: *"Five! Count 'em — five, and no burning!"* — after which nets and saps, and a PC at 0 wakes in the Grey Fee's hold instead of dying).
- **The letter.** Writing box, false bottom, Grey Fee's stern cabin: **Investigation DC 14.** Or **Perception DC 16** on Tarrow's body (tarred wallet, reader's copy). Or **Intimidation DC 13 / Persuasion DC 15** on a prisoner. **Failure floor:** Roke searches the dead himself, says nothing for two days, then puts it on the table and asks what he has been carrying.
- **What it gives them:** they are hunted and have been since Loftwick; it is the same courier network; the hunter is guessing; and the paper is worth more than the people (six hundred a head alive, *whatever you care to ask* for the documents).

## Encounter 3 — The Grey-Back (Day 37)

**One giant shark and three hunter sharks. 3,150 XP.** *(Six PCs: a fourth hunter, +450.)* Plant the superstition on day 31 and let Roke dismiss it.

- It attacks **the ship**, not the party, and rams at the end of any round in which nothing is bleeding in the water.
- **Hull track, four segments.** Heavy steering (DC 12 to hold a course) → sprung rudder (sweeps only, four hands occupied) → a started plank (two on the pump to Hardby) → taking water faster than the pump. Segment 4 is not a fail state; it is a different arrival, and the customs mole looks at her repairs instead of her bales.
- **Two rounds per man in the water.** Wick Ferrin goes over on the first strike — name him on day nineteen, and let him have taught Elle to splice. A party that kills sharks loses him; a party that saves him eats two more rams. There is no clever third answer.
- **The bait is the smart win.** Salt cod in the hold, staved over the lee side, ends the fight. Award the full XP: a table that solves a monster with cargo has done the thing this whole intermission teaches.
- **Underwater rules to have ready:** submerged creatures have **resistance to fire**; ranged weapon attacks are at disadvantage inside normal range and auto-miss beyond it; melee without a swim speed is at disadvantage except dagger, javelin, shortsword, spear, trident. Do not invent a lightning-in-water bonus — it becomes an argument in Phase 8.
- **This one belongs to Kitty**, the only person aboard who can meet it in its element.

## XP and the sixth-level line

The party ends Phase 2 on **12,169** each; 6th level is **14,000**; they need **1,831 each**, i.e. **9,155 XP of enemies** across five PCs.

| Build | Enemy XP | Per PC | Lands at |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Base (6 ankhegs / boarding as written / shark + 3 hunters) | 8,700 | 1,740 | 13,909 — *91 short* |
| + the boarding's second wave | 9,100 | 1,820 | 13,989 — *11 short* |
| + second wave **and** a fourth hunter shark | 9,550 | 1,910 | **14,079 — 6th at the rail** |

Run both dials, or award the voyage as a milestone the way the Loftwick sessions were awarded. **Do not** hand the level out on arrival in Hardby; levelling on the deck is what makes six weeks feel earned.

Budgets, five PCs at 5th: low 2,500 / moderate 5,000 / high 7,500. All three sit between low and moderate on paper and play at moderate, because each is an objective with a clock and this party has no front line to absorb a first exchange.

## Hand-off to Phase 3

Two sentences of *Arrival — Commercial Cover* want a hand:

- **"The harbour takes you in without a flicker."** If the hull track reached segment 2 or worse, it takes them in with one flicker — the customs boy counts bales, then walks aft, looks at the repairs, and writes down whatever they tell him. He still does not count faces. But there is now a piece of paper in a harbour office with their story on it.
- **"You have learned the smell of raw fleece in a wet hold."** Let that carry the rest: nine dead pirates, a fortnight of pumping, and the hand they got back or didn't.

They come up the Whalemarket steps with three things they did not have in Loftwick — a circular proving they were hunted before they left Keoland, a mark in wax tying that hunt to Tannery Row, and one initial nobody has explained — plus **the third wharf** and **trade-drafts on a Hardby house that is not Cindren & Vhal**.

Everything Phase 3 depends on is untouched: the firm still suspects without knowing, Tamsin is still alive at Whitemoor, Veska is still cautious, Tarsh is still confrontable, and Castrian Vell is still the host. If the Grey Fee escaped, that is worth one Pressure Clock segment at most.

## What this intermission deliberately does not do

- It does not name, hint at, or narrow **"E."** beyond one letter of the alphabet. No forbidden name appears on any page, player-facing or otherwise.
- It does not advance the conspiracy. Three new facts, all of them about the party's own exposure.
- It does not gate anything. Every clue has three routes and a floor; every fight can be ended early by a clever table.
