*GM only. Full spoilers, first line to last. This is the narrative spine of the whole campaign — the story as designed, the three plotlines that carry it, who the players of the conspiracy are, and the order in which your party is meant to encounter it all. ## The Truth, Stated Once

**"E." is Eclavdra of House Eilserv — drow, of Erelhei-Cinlu.** She is running a decade-scale positioning operation on the surface, decades ahead of the events of *Vault of the Drow*: cultivating commercial allies whose money, cover, and logistics serve House Eilserv's much slower move. Her instruments, in descending order of knowledge, are: the **under-road courier network** (Kethren's kind — hers), the **handler layer** (Korre, Quill, Kestrel & Reed — paid and compartmentalized), the **commercial layer** (Cindren & Vhal, embodied by Tarlith Vhal Sereth — advised by annotation, never met), and the **political layer** (the harbor-commission play, aimed at the Aerdy succession).

**Blooming Rot itself — the agricultural collapse — was an engineered proof-of-concept.** Grain destroyed on schedule; insurance claims filed *before* the losses; public panic measured and priced ("surface panic acceptable: yes"); recovery rehearsed. And the under-road the party finally walks was built by no one living: it is an ancient descent **rented through the paperwork of a dead Aerdy bureaucracy** — the Permissive Survey, Class VII — and the horror of the campaign's second half is that the paperwork still works.

The campaign's hinge is Caelith's Letter 6, delivered near the end of Phase 4, and it is meant to be quoted back at you: **"The conspiracy you have named is not the conspiracy."**

What Eclavdra wants on the surface is simple and patient: **three commissioners seated on Rel Astra's harbor commission before the next Aerdy Overking is named.** "Harbor before throne" is the whole strategy in three words — whoever owns those seats owns the eastern revenue when the succession resolves, and the succession is the moment House Eilserv intends to be positioned for. Everything else — Haskur, the grain fraud, the murders, the leak in Caelith's office — is scaffolding around that calendar.

## The Three Plotlines

You built three complete plotlines, and all three end at Eclavdra. Here they are, restored — what each one is, who carries it, the order of its links, where it tops out, and which phase it owns. The design principle to hold onto: **the first two plotlines are built to complete and feel insufficient. Only the third one physically reaches her.**

### Plotline One — THE MONEY (who paid)

*The forensic thread. Dominant in Phases 2–4. Completable — and completed — on the surface.*

The chain, in order: the **letter of credit** (drawn six weeks before the fight; "Hold for V., or his agent of record") → the **crane-and-coins seal** → named as **Cindren & Vhal, Bonded Factors** (Records Alcove, Phase 2) → **Gilst's bearer instrument** on the C&V *Hardby* chop (the smoking gun in the lozenge tin) → **Solen Mereth's** clearing-chop and second set of books (Hardby, Phase 3) → **Tarlith Vhal Sereth's** marginal-endorsement hand, named by Veska's notarial opinion → the **three commissioner candidates** his money and pressure are seating (Rel Astra, Phase 4) → and, above Sereth, only an annotation: **the firm answers to a hand it has never met.**

Its carriers: Caelith and Trina (Loftwick), Veska Maelan (Hardby — the one person who can read the chops), Therion Halask and Belven Astor (Rel Astra — the advocates who turn evidence into procedure).

**Its ceiling:** Sereth. He is the first name a court can use and the last name the money reaches. In the Path-2 confrontation he says it himself — *"The annotations are not from this side of the great desert… The notes were always correct. I do not ask further."* The money plotline convicts a senior partner and collapses the harbor play for two years, and the table should feel, at that exact moment of triumph, that they have named a glove.

### Plotline Two — THE PEOPLE (who knew)

*The conscience thread. Runs through everything; owns Phase 5. Every witness knows exactly one layer up.*

The chain, in order: **Merro Gilst** (dead bonds clerk; four years of shadow retainers; the fear note — "the third winter I have been afraid") → **Tamsin Moraven** (the grain factor who queried the claims-before-loss pattern and vanished for it) → **Reln Pollow** (the physician whose certifications are the conspiracy's paperwork of death) → **Hesren Vesh** (the courteous junior clerk whose innocent courier message summons the knife) → the three candidates' **three modes of capture** — Mirelth *owned*, Vesh *leveraged*, Tenrel *willing* (Phase 4's civics lesson) → **Ostren Pell and Sera** (the leak in Caelith's own office, coerced through his sister — Phase 5's ethical engine) → and at the bottom of the world, **Merrit Osk** in a witness cell, whose statement ends "My mother is in the Free Quarter. She lives at fourteen Cooper's Lane."

Its carriers: the victims and the almost-guilty — plus Trina, whose network of school fees and salon regulars is how the party keeps finding the human beings inside the ledgers.

**Its ceiling:** every interrogation ends with some version of *"below," "east,"* or *"I never met him."* Sarth won't name her broker; Resh trades everything except his Rel Astra contact; Pell knows only "the man on the lane"; Korre never asked; Kethren says "the road is below." When every witness ends at the same wall, the wall is the clue.

### Plotline Three — THE ROAD (how it moves)

*The descent thread. Nearly invisible in Phases 1–4, surfacing in 5, dominant from 6 on. The only plotline that never dead-ends — because it is the one that goes to her.*

The chain, in order: the **black-lacquered relay tube** (courier-grade, forget-seal, "elven but peculiar" — Phase 2's quiet opening move) → the **forged Audit Hall seal** on Sarth's writ (the first thread of the courier artery) → **Pell → Trell → Vesten Quill → Kestrel & Reed** (the leak walked upward, Phase 5) → **Korre and the Brass Crow**, box 17, the eleventh-bell transfer in the Old Stonecistern (Phase 6 — Haskur handed over as cargo) → the **Ashen Itinerary**: *"No sail. No road. VII Stair."* → the **triangulation** that turns the phrase into a place (Phase 7) → **Tarnsmere Toll and the Permission Door** — token, phrase, tally, witness (Phase 8) → **Witness Station Seven and the Black Ledger Gate** (Phase 9), where Warden Instruction 6 delivers the campaign's driest punchline: the route's deeper figures respond only to formally filed queries. **You cannot knock on Eclavdra's door. You have to file.**

Its carriers: Aldea Veren (who reads matter), Iren Velash (who reads paper), Edrik Vone (who sells routes), Dalmor Vetch (who remembers stairs), and the enemy's own couriers — Kethren above all.

**Its ceiling: none.** That is the point. The money ends at Sereth; the people end at walls; the road just keeps descending.

### How the Braids Hand Off

If you remember nothing else about the order of the campaign, remember this sequence of dominance:

1. **Phases 1–2 — Money leads.** Objects become names; names become addresses; the road plotline is planted as one strange tube nobody can pursue yet.
2. **Phases 3–4 — Money completes; People deepen.** Hardby gives the machine faces; Rel Astra convicts one. The party wins the nameable conspiracy and is handed Letter 6 telling them it wasn't the conspiracy.
3. **Phase 5 — People lead.** With the surface case closed, the campaign turns inward: the leak, the five choices for Pell, and Aldea's reply — the first evidence that points **down** instead of east.
4. **Phases 6–7 — The Road takes over.** Greyhawk converts the courier artery into a route; five interpreters turn a phrase into a place.
5. **Phases 8–9 — The Road is all there is.** The door, the stair, the station, the gate, the fork. No boss fight. Paperwork all the way down.

## Phase One — Loftwick's Return

*The opening evening and the morning after. Investigative, DC cap 16. The phase's three jobs: prove Haskur survived by design; make the party love Caelith; get the commission accepted.*

The party comes back from killing **Haskur Vandrell** in the wilderness and is welcomed into **Loftwick** — deliberately at the Lesser Gate, not the triumphal one, because **Caelith Dunivar**, Keeper of Foreign Correspondence, is stage-managing their arrival into the *durable* civic record. On the Little Palace steps they are named **Friends of the Common Defense** and given the sealed wooden tokens that will move clerks and gaolers for the rest of the campaign. (Your table did all of this the long way around — the Forbidden Orc Keep, Kael'thorne wearing Mayor Thornwell, the letters in the cottage signed E. Treat those cottage letters as **E.-observation zero**: they establish, before the module's own sequence even starts, that E. runs assets that are neither banks nor humans.)

The load-bearing scene is the **private audience**: door closed, no scribes, a brass token on the desk he does not yet explain. Caelith's speech lands four facts — Haskur's name has crossed his desk twice on papers nobody issued; the man was "reduced to nothing" and is breathing somewhere anyway; a **letter of credit countersigned in Rel Astra, dated six weeks before the fight**, sits in the strongroom; and Caelith will not pursue it through his own office because he is not certain his office is closed at every door. Then the **strongroom seven**, by candlelight: the torn spellbook; the sealed wallet; the signet ring; the letter of credit ("Hold for V., or his agent of record" — the grammar of banking closing around a resurrection); the ritual focus; the brass map tube with the small **"R."** beside a roadhouse a day south; the Rel Astran trade slug. The crane-and-coins mark appears three times and is **never named in Phase 1** — the name is the Records Alcove's payoff.

Overnight, one complication from the d6 menu (your best long-game options: the **Burning Page**, which pre-seeds the Pell leak, or the **Cook's Niece**, which puts the working class downstream of the conspiracy). At breakfast, the phase closes itself: among the morning post sits an envelope under the same crane-and-coins seal — *"We hope your visit to Loftwick is brief."* The party is already known, and the knower does not want them coming east. Caelith asks once more; on acceptance he hands over the letter of introduction to **Veska Maelan** (Hardby trade-clearing notary), a pouch of Yeomanry trade slugs, and one unexplained name, **Holyn Sevarian**.

**In order:** approach and escort → public welcome and tokens → settling in → private audience → the strongroom seven → one first-night complication → the breakfast envelope and the commission.

**Leave with:** the seven items seen once (at night — daylight re-reads them in Phase 2), the unnamed crane-and-coins, the "R." noted or not, a relationship with Caelith, and an accepted commission.

## Phase Two — The Dead Man's Receipts

*The current phase at your table. Two or three Loftwick days. Investigative and political, one possible short combat. DC cap 18. Jobs: prove institutional scale; put Cindren & Vhal at the center on paper; force a departure east under one of three travel approaches.*

Phase 2 runs on the clean-motion spine — three acts, two street-hubs, and a chase — with the six-segment **Pressure Clock** face-up on the table throughout.

**Act One — The Table.** The morning evidence review: the seven items again, in daylight, on numbered linen, with **Trina Alvere** at the table ("I advise Caelith on people"). Caelith lays the brass token down and discloses the **Hand of the Duke** — the part of his office that goes outside Yeomanry borders — and the pattern: Haskur has been pulled back from death **twice before** (Onnwal 575, a Hardby tavern 580). Daylight re-reads convert objects into leads: the focus is *burned*, its wax sigil (a three-rayed sun closing into a fist) **not in Haskur's hand** — someone else laid the contingency; the false letter of passage is an **inside job** on real vellum with a retired Bureau chief's real signature; the clipped coin's countermark resolves toward C&V's internal clearing-mark; the lacquered tube is a **relay** — courier-grade, nested for deniability, a one-shot *Forget* on the seal, interior wiped, heat-reactive residue, craftsmanship "elven but peculiar" that no sage can place; the vellum strip says **HARBOR BEFORE THRONE**; the seed pouch is show-grain for false crop claims; and the load-bearing **ledger fragment** — *VANDRELL settled / MORAVEN delayed / GILST transferred* — three hands, no sums, no dates. Accounting verbs applied to human beings: **people carried as accounts.** That is the campaign's thesis in six words, and it will be said again, literally, in Phase 9. The Records Alcove then turns three names into **two streets and a firm**: Cindren & Vhal (satellite on Wick Lane, houses in Hardby and Rel Astra); Merro Gilst (dead, rooms on Wick Lane, death certified by Reln Pollow, also Wick Lane); Tamsin Moraven (missing, counting house on Tannerway).

**Act Two — The City.** Two hubs, player-ordered, and every thread runs east. **Wick Lane** is the dense, kinetic hub: Hesren Vesh at the C&V satellite ("reads like *Hardby* work" — and his dutiful courier message afterward is what triggers the knife); **Gilst's rooms** above the stationer's, the load-bearing search — the hollowed *Constitutional Commentaries* holding the receipts book (forty-one entries from "H.V. Hardby," **three countersigned "E."**), the throat-lozenge tin with its false floor and the **bearer instrument on the C&V Hardby chop, same hand as Vandrell's escrow** (the single most forward-pointing object in the act — "We now have the hand. The hand is in Hardby."), the locket, the burned "Do not copy eastern marks twice," the coin shaving, and the fear note; **Pollow**, more frightened than a routine certification warrants — grant him his two days or he is poisoned; and **the Sparrow's Run**, the chase — Anla "Sparrow" Coyle, sixteen, a hired lookout who has been logging everyone who visits the stationer's door, bolts across her own ground and *cannot be caught by design*, shedding the oilcloth fold at reach two so the clue survives every outcome: a surveillance log carrying the party's own descriptions, a dead-letter drop to stake out, and the line **"east on the Sevenday cart"** beside a C&V-kin routing glyph. **Tannerway** is the human hub: Doril Veth at the counting house, red-eyed, with the carbons stolen and the **claims-before-loss** pattern (eleven days, three weeks, two days early — "That is not theft. That is being paid to know what tomorrow looks like."); Vellin Moraven with the Sevenday note and the realization that **Ailen is in Hardby right now**; Sergeant Anwic, honest and thin-filed. Six independent vectors — the chop, the "Hardby work" line, the cart, the queries, the daughter, and Veska's standing introduction — all point down one road. Let the players draw the arrow themselves.

**Act Three — The Squeeze and the Road.** **Arthen Moll** arrives — the polite deputy with 1,200 gp per head and the sincere belief that loud strangers cost the Yeomanry money; decline him and the same coordination that sent the deputy sends the thief. **Merev Sarth's** retrieval night on the strongroom is a professional's burglary against a prepared defense (the chest holds decoys; the originals moved to Trina's the moment Vesh's courier left): captured, she eventually yields the Hardby cutout name **"Tarsh"**; killed or searched, she yields the **forged Audit Hall seal** Caelith has hunted in his own office for nine months — the Pell thread, not to be named for three more phases. Caelith's conclusion pivots the phase ("…by nightfall they will know we learned it"), and the **three travel approaches** end it: Official Delegation (authority), Commercial Cover (cover — Trina's letter to Castrian Vell), Quiet Pursuit (distance). *"I can give you authority, cover, or distance. I cannot give you all three."* At the Lesser Gate he hands the lead rider the brass Hand-token and a small folded paper: **17 Cooper's Tale, Hardby** — the Mossen Place, the safe house, read on the road.

**In order:** morning review and Hand reveal → the seven daylight items (ledger fragment last) → Records Alcove (names become two streets) → the hubs in any order, chase included → Moll → Sarth → Caelith's conclusion → the three approaches → departure and the folded paper.

**Leave with (the five must-knows):** Haskur survived by design; he is a node in a logistics network, not a lone agent; Cindren & Vhal sits at the center and the live hand is in Hardby; Loftwick will not let this finish here; the road is Loftwick → Hardby → Rel Astra and it must be taken with urgency. Plus, collected-not-resolved: the "E." countersigns, the tube's craft, HARBOR BEFORE THRONE, and the leak.

**The one honest red herring:** the roadhouse "R." leads to the Sign of the Brown Hare, **Rosalin Brent** — Haskur's half-sister — and a cedar box of a dead man's childhood. No conspiracy content at all; priced at about five days and two clock segments; pays out only humanity, and the never-sent letter that lands again in Phase 6. It teaches the table that not every mark is a door. Contain it to one session and let it be worth it.

## Phase Three — The Hardby Investigation

*The middle link. Denser than Phases 1 and 2 combined; one real combat sequence (the Tamsin recovery) and possibly a second (the clock-5 squad). DC cap 17, except the Solen turn at 22. Jobs: name the broker; resolve Tamsin; produce the document that puts Sereth inside the conspiracy in his own hand.*

The party arrives in **Hardby** — merchant Free City on the Wild Coast, governed by the tired old **Gynarchy** — under whichever approach they chose, and the approach is the load-bearing variable all phase: it decides how Tamsin's thread resolves, whether Tarsh is reachable in person, and which door into the C&V branch is open.

**The Mossen Place first.** 17 Cooper's Tale: the key from **Mistress Betha Halver** at the corner grocer ("the cellarer's apprentice came down from Loftwick to mind the house"), the dust-sheets, the two floorboard caches — maps, wax, 200 gp, and Caelith's sealed letter *"To whoever finds this,"* which names the phase's three resources: **Jaron Pell** (the C&V branch's bonded front-desk clerk, paid to be civil and to send a runner to Rel Astra at the first meaningful inquiry), **Veska Maelan** (the notary who matters), and **Brother Wenra Holst** (the discreet infirmary). *"I have not left a weapon in the house. I have left a place to put a weapon down."* Mistress Halver's gossip then quietly seeds the whole phase: Jaron Pell is unhappy in his work; a senior partner "who never lets anyone see his hands" has come down from Rel Astra twice in six weeks; a thin smoker-voiced man drinks at the **Coopered Wreck** at the head of Black Cog Lane with his back to the wall; and **Tamsin Moraven got off a wagon at the eastern caravan yard fifteen days ago**, tired, alone.

**The Veska reveal is the load-bearing scene.** At her Whalemarket Quay office (or, on the delegation path, through her apprentice Hella Voren's smuggled packet), she reads the party's objects like a professional and converts them into **four people**: **Solen Mereth**, head of bond-loading at the Hardby branch — he chopped the escrow, he kept the defect-stamp, he runs the second set of books; **Tarlith Vhal Sereth**, the reclusive Rel Astra senior partner whose smaller, older hand wrote the marginal endorsement — "the one whose name a court must hear"; **Anver Resh**, freelance broker, formerly Solen's clerk — the man who, as "Tarsh," hired Sarth; and **a fourth hand she cannot place** — E.-observations one through three: the loop, the serif, the angle of the descender. The **defect-stamp** is physical perjury: a chipped clearing-stamp Veska herself certified destroyed in 579, still stamping paper in the branch strongroom tonight. Her prepared **notarial opinion** is the critical handout of the campaign's first half — the bridge that makes Phase 4 prosecutable.

**Tarsh at the Coopered Wreck** (in person on the two quiet approaches): shown evidence, he trades everything he has — Solen's second set since 580, Sereth's seal, Tamsin's location, his payment chain — for a one-way passport and his name off the indictment. Everything except one thing: *"That man is not someone I will trade for a passport."* His Rel Astra contact stays in his hand. Another ceiling; another arrow east.

**Tamsin resolves by approach.** Quiet Pursuit: alive in a Black Cog Lane warehouse behind the shell company Greenwall & Mast, guarded by rotating thugs under **Reyna Worth** — who will kill her on a botched rescue; night raid while Worth is home. Commercial Cover: moved to the coastal **Whitemoor Estate**, five hired blades *(six PCs: six)* under **Olo Crask**, a professional who will not kill her without explicit order; infiltrate by sea ahead of the next courier. Official Delegation: murdered three days before arrival on Solen's confirmation of the filing — the body claimed from the harbor, and her smuggled **last letter** read aloud at the table. It surfaces **Joren Krill**, the weighmaster she almost trusted and crossed out, and it is the beat you treat with the gravity it deserves.

**The Gynarchy Registry turns the slogan into a calendar.** Through **Zoria Weis** — Caelith's called-in debt, not a friend — the harbor-commission registry shows Rel Astra's three old-house seats all vacant by death, on schedule: **First Seat, Lord Hessar Olm, 582, natural causes** (the conspiracy got lucky once); **Third Seat, Lady Mirin Vesh, 583, a fall on her own staircase** (Worth's work); **Fifth Seat, Master Ervan Dale, 584, a brief illness** (the procedural-kill template). Whoever seats three commissioners before the next Overking is named owns the eastern revenue. The party leaves knowing the play but not yet the candidates.

**The C&V branch on Counting House Lane is the indictment package.** Standing staff: Mira Cindren (junior partner, kept ignorant), Solen, **Berrin Onn** (vault clerk, honest), **Dren Marsh** (night guard, yields to the brass token), **Vella Tannin** (junior bond-officer whose brother died under a falsified bonded escrow — the sympathetic-clerk path), Jaron Pell out front — and Hesren Vesh, supervised above a chandler, if Loftwick's clock recalled him; extract him or he is murdered two weeks after you leave. Four targets in priority order: the **second set of books**; the **blotter rubbing** of Sereth's signature; the **defect-stamp**; Solen's **cipher key**. Three doors in, keyed to approach — burglary, Vella's copy-read (extract her within 24 hours afterward, and name her brother a victim in the indictment), or the subpoena that yields notarized rubbings and the legally damning existence of a sealed branch-level escrow cabinet. And one soft path across all of them: **turning Solen Mereth** — DC 22, Veska's opinion in hand, a private and procedurally correct setting — for a sealed packet delivered the morning after he resigns.

Interleaved: Caelith's three letters (the Closure motion is coming; the office-leak name travels by separate pouch; *do not carry Veska's original east*). The Hardby Pressure Clock runs faster than Loftwick's; expect to leave at 4–5 with the harbor trying to close under you.

**In order:** arrival by approach → the Mossen Place and Halver's gossip → first contact (Castrian Vell on cover; Veska directly on pursuit; Hella's packet on delegation) → the Veska reveal → Tarsh at the Wreck → the Tamsin recovery → the Gynarchy registry → the C&V branch operation → departure east (the Lacquer Wave, the coast road, or the diplomatic carriage).

**Leave with:** the copied indictment package (Veska's opinion, second-set ledgers or carbons, the blotter rubbing, the defect-stamp), three named people and one unplaceable hand, the harbor-before-throne calendar, and Ailen and every extractable witness accounted for.

## Phase Four — The Rel Astra Confrontation

*The long climax of the surface arc. Investigative and political; the only large combat is the Path-3 contingency you hope never to run. DCs cap 18/17. Party ~levels 7–11; the clock has four segments and starts at +2 on the delegation path. Jobs: name the three candidates; run the Sereth confrontation; plant E.-observations four and five.*

Rel Astra is the city where the clocks all chime together. Within a day the party finds **Therion Halask** at the Brass Sextant coffee-house — *"By the wheatsheaf and the open scroll"* — and the retired advocate becomes the phase's procedural engine: he takes custody of the indictment package and will not let it leave his keeping; he sequences, he does not delay. He names the board: **three vacant seats, three candidates, three modes of capture.** **Lord Faren Mirelth** is *owned* — bankrupt since 578, carrying 20,000 gp of C&V "personal advances," a textbook §187 disqualification the moment the loans are documented; he would honestly rather withdraw quietly. **Magister Andrune Vesh** is *leveraged* — an honest technocrat whose sister-in-law **Lyla** faces twenty years on her late husband's customs forgeries, with C&V's fixers dangling the case; nothing disqualifies him, so the only clean move is to **remove the pressure** (Halask can demolish the case for ~800 gp and two weeks — pay it, and Vesh withdraws while Halask stays fully committed). **Mistress Coriath Tenrel** is *willing* — she approached Sereth herself, eighteen months ago, votes for ambition, and can only be stopped by stopping the seating itself: indict Sereth, or brief **Captain Volin Reach** through the imperial channel so the vote dies 2–2. Run all three and the party can read capture anywhere for the rest of the campaign. Do all three moves — disqualify, indict, brief — and the harbor play collapses for at least two years.

Around the spine: **Belven Astor** at the Wave-and-Anchor chandlery, the one reliable councilor, coordinating the aftermath and warning the party off **Solvard Mein**; the optional **Galenix Naelax** audience — the Aerdy claimant under polite house-arrest who owes C&V 38,000 gp, has never met a single conspirator, and does not yet understand his debt has already been called (brief him honestly, leave him alone, or hand him the package and let him buy his freedom with it — none of the three is wrong); and the mid-phase **imperial registry** find, **E.-observation four**: the Third Seat's succession-deferral, filed within days of Mirin Vesh's inquest, annotated in the unplaceable hand — *"Sufficient. Hold open until the second autumn after the next."* E. does not just fund the conspiracy. **E. sets its calendar, in writing, years ahead** — under a hard dark wax that leaves black residue and matches nothing imperial.

When the clock reaches its witness-pressure segment, the conspiracy's answer is fire: the **chandlery arson beat** — an interceptable, telegraphed attempt on Astor's records (a Bandit Captain and three Thugs at ~7th level, calibrated for your five *(six PCs: a fourth Thug)*), with a captured lead who can be walked back to the Cindren-aligned advocate quietly seeding rumors against Halask. It is the one fight the middle of the phase wants, and stopping it protects Path 1's paper trail.

**The Sereth confrontation is the load-bearing scene.** The room is the message — a serious party with a real package gets the private working office, the light falling on the visitors and not on him. Three resolutions, all legitimate: **Path 1, indictment** in Aerdi commercial court — Halask files, Ostrach denies the motions to dismiss, the depositions run under retaliation pressure, the commission suspends candidate selection, and ninety days later Sereth is convicted, stripped, and excluded for twenty years; **Path 2, private removal** — the package used as leverage for his resignation, his candidates' withdrawal (he refuses Tenrel's, because she was never his to withdraw — notice how thin the control really is), the Mirelth originals, one Hardby correspondent's name, a signed lie that protects the firm — and, if asked directly about the annotations, **E.-observation five**, in his own voice: *"The annotations are not from this side of the great desert. I have not met the writer… The notes were always correct. I do not ask further."* The named villain of the first act confesses that he, too, reports upward into a void; **Path 3, kinetic removal** — the worst outcome, wanted bulletins, the strongroom emptied to Rauxes, the trail cold, and Caelith's most measured letter. If the party tries to humiliate Sereth, he does not get angry; he gets less generous.

Caelith's letters land through the phase: Trina has sent a sealed copy of Veska's opinion to *"my old correspondent in the wood"*; the Closure motion passed and Caelith now writes on his own paper; *"The clerk in my office is named Ostren Pell. I have known this for nineteen days. I have done nothing about it"*; Haskur has been seen in Greyhawk with a younger man whose gait is "not Aerdi and not Yeomanry"; and, read slowly at the table, Letter 6: **"The conspiracy you have named is not the conspiracy."** The phase ends with five E.-observations on file, no face, and the departure read-aloud's last three words: *You ride west.*

**In order:** arrival by route → Halask at the Brass Sextant → the Three Seats (Mirelth, then Vesh, then Tenrel) → Astor's chandlery → the imperial registry annotation → the arson intercept when the clock bites → (optionally) Galenix → the Sereth confrontation → Letters 4–6 → departure west.

**Leave with:** Sereth resolved (indicted, resigned, or dead); the slate broken or bruised (Mirelth out, Vesh out if Lyla was freed, Tenrel isolated or seated); Galenix's disposition; five observations of the hand; and the hinge line in the players' own mouths.

## Phase Five — The Small Matter

*The quiet final act of the main arc: counterintelligence at home. Administrative horror, not combat — the only fight is Vesten Quill, CR 2, if it happens at all. Six to eight in-game days against a nine-day political fuse. Jobs: show what changed; resolve the leak; deliver Aldea's reply; commit the party to a vector.*

The Loftwick they return to is not the one that named them Friends of the Common Defense. The Civic Way is thin, the Audit Hall doors are shut, and **the brass quill pin is gone from Caelith's lapel** — Brane's Closure motion has passed 4–3, his title is rescinded pending a review that ends with a binding Council vote on **Day 9**. Behind a barred door he lays out four papers: the motion; the leak — **"His name is Ostren Pell. I have known for three weeks… the man who pays him is holding his sister"**; the still-sealed reply from Trina's correspondent in the wood; and the Greyhawk affidavit — Haskur, alive, at the door of a counting-house called the **Brass Crow**, beside a younger man whose gait was not Aerdi and not Yeomanry. *"Sereth was not the hand. He was a glove."*

**The debrief opens the packet.** **Aldea Veren's reply** is the descent thread's first hard proof, three findings each fatal to a surface explanation: the **wax** matches no surface recipe she has ever documented; the **hand** is court-trained but not court-raised — taught from documents that *came up to the writer*, ascenders pushed in the wrong stroke order, a student who never sat in the room; and the **geographic impossibility** — the earliest dated annotation (Hardby, Lent 574, on paper milled in Rel Astra in autumn 573) could not have been written by anyone reachable from any sailing port in the window. She will not write what she thinks. Asked in person, with exactly the right narrow question — *"Could the writer have learned this hand from someone who has never been to the surface?"* — she gives the party a suspicion they will carry, unconfirmed, in their heads until Phase 8–9. This is the first arrow that points **down**.

**The five choices for Pell** are the campaign's purest ethics engine, and Caelith refuses to direct: expose him (Sera safe, channel burned, Caelith embarrassed); **turn him** (a knowing double agent — but then Sera's protection is the party's daily, practical job); **feed the channel** (the False-Packet Menu — five lies with priced consequences, and the one Caelith admits he would choose); **shadow the chain** (never touch Pell; the best option if they can do it invisibly); remove him (the worst, unless Sera is extracted first). And the unmentioned sixth, which the system answers without endorsing. The **courier-chain crawl** is Plotline Three surfacing where the party can walk it, packet by packet: the dead-drop brick in the civic copy room → **Trell** crossing Cooper's lane on Tuesday and Friday mornings → the courier desk → the rented room above the printer's on **Quill Street**, tenant "V. Quill" → the lead-pipe drop behind the Hall of Bonds, yielding the receipt signed with a single **"V."** Take **Vesten Quill** quietly on Day 8 and he names the layer above him — a Hardby-aligned letter agency operating out of a **Greyhawk City** address — and knows nothing further. The artery, walked upward, tops out pointing north.

**The political front runs in parallel** toward the Day 9 vote: **Brane** cannot be bought, threatened, or shamed — he is *right enough* — only outmaneuvered; **Councilor Edda Halvern** can be moved by funding the southern militia's 5,000 gp from a non-Audit-Hall source (Trina quietly puts up 3,000 if asked properly, and the deal must be presented by Caelith or Voss, never by the party); **Councilor Mairra Voss** will sponsor a renewed vote on a clean documentary case, and the courier receipt is exactly that. Move Halvern and arm Voss and the motion fails 3–4; the pin may come back by courier. Fail, and Caelith goes silent and the campaign continues through private channels. Either way, the phase ends with **Letter 7** sealed for one day's ride and a public or private commitment to one of four vectors: **A — Greyhawk City** (the Brass Crow trail; this is the campaign's canonical spine), **B — the wood correspondent** (Aldea in person, originals in hand), **C — the counter-leak** (Quill, Sera, the drop), or **D — the Rel Astra legal continuation** (Path-1 tables only). *"We have more doors than hands. Choose your door."*

**In order:** the return and the barred door → the framing speech → the Day 2 debrief and Aldea's reply → the Pell decision → (as chosen) the false packet and the courier crawl, with Sera protected throughout → the political front → the Quill Street take on Day 8 → the Day 9 vote → Letter 7 and the road out.

**Leave with:** the leak resolved in one of five shapes; Aldea's three findings in hand and her unwritten suspicion, maybe, in their heads; the receipt; the Council vote decided; and a chosen vector — with Greyhawk waiting up every road that matters.

## Postscript — Phases Six through Nine, in Capsule

*The expansion arc: the road plotline, walked to the bottom of its first flight. Full run detail lives in the phase masters; this is the story.*

### Phase Six — The Brass Crow (Greyhawk City)

Six nodes, any three of which converge on the climax: **Tellan Verth** at the Hand of Coals, who has been waiting to say "I have been waiting for you"; surveillance on the **Brass Crow** counting-house, where the registry shows **H. V. Andren** — and beside the name, the smallest mark in the phase and the most important one in the campaign: **a vertical stroke crossed at three-quarter height**, the foreign-correspondent side-mark. It is **Kethren's courier mark**, and it will reappear on every registry fragment below Tarnsmere; when a player recognizes it again at the Permission Door, the campaign clicks shut like a well-made box. Then **Iren Velash** in the Garden Quarter — the keystone reading, rewarded for bringing everything at once: *"This is not a sea-route term. It is a descent term… older than the city. You will not find it on a port chart."*; **Edrik Vone's** purchasable ledgers (Korre's traffic, the pending eleventh-bell pickup); **Kestrel & Reed** on Hawkstone Way, where **Doras Kemmel** turns for real protection and box 17 has already given up one packet to a young man in grey-and-bone; and **Veyra Korre's** rented room on Cordwainer's Row, with the burned strips ("…clean hands drown first…") and the slip where "VII Stair" is struck through in her hand and rewritten, smaller and more careful, in his.

It climaxes at the **eleventh-bell transfer in the Old Stonecistern** beneath a derelict bathhouse — not the Underdark, Greyhawk infrastructure, a rehearsal for descent without being one. Haskur — alive, diminished, *being moved* — is handed from Korre's surface network to Kethren's route while two agents stand ready to burn what does not travel. Whatever the party wins or loses in that room, three things survive every outcome: the fresh **route mark** (seven descending cuts beneath a black hill), the **Ashen Itinerary** — *"No sail. No road. VII Stair."* and *"Blooming was proof. Rot was proof. Recovery was the question."* — and the ladder of ignorance reaching its top rung. Korre is the person the conspiracy is willing to lose; Kethren is the person it is not. Haskur's own line, if taken: *"I was not rescued. I was collected."* The villain of the wilderness is freight. Caelith's unsigned morning note: *"The city was the desk. You have found the drawer."*

### Phase Seven — The Seventh Question (turning a phrase into a place)

Five interpretive sources, five categories — name, function, permission, material, place — so no single roll or NPC carries the reveal. **Iren**: VII is a route-class of *permitted descents* in obsolete Aerdy mining law; "no sail, no road" is a literal transit condition. **Aldea**: the wax is mine-seal work; the stone dust is southern Cairn Hills; the "hill" is a surveyor's convention for a **sealed entrance**. **Vone's route-exception ledger**: the VII account prefix on Korre's traffic, destination **Tarnsmere Toll**, and his own breadcrumb — *T·S·C → Tarnsmere Spur Cairn → Guild sheet 47 → confirm with surveyor.* **The Guild and Dalmor Vetch**: claim VII/0341 with the claimant's name physically scraped and the Survey seal re-waxed seven years after the Survey was dissolved; the missing surveyor **Garrold Eft**, out since 542; and Vetch's living glossary — *"Miners call it a stair when the earth charges first"* — plus his sketched map with its perfect closing line: *"If the seventh cut is gone, you are too late, or you are at the wrong cairn."* **Any prisoner** adds the courier's koan: seven cuts, beneath a hill that is not a hill, northeast. The conspiracy's own **False Stair** (six cuts, one oak, paper too clean) proves the real marker by exclusion even if swallowed whole. The phase ends at the **Seven-Cut Cairn** above the ruined tollhouse: hand the rubbing across the table and let it match the cistern sketch. The stair begins where the maps learned to lie.

### Phase Eight — The Stair Beneath Tarnsmere (the Permission Door)

The threshold dungeon, and the argument made architecture. Tarnsmere Toll reverses every assumption — **the toll window faces downhill; the tollhouse watched for people coming back up.** Seventeen tally cuts hide behind a vanished coat-rack, the bottom three recent. The registry fragment reads *"No sail. No road. Toll paid beneath"* over admissions with **"Return: nil"** — two of them halfling cousins-of-record, the payoff of the halfling-network thread (Cam's, now Elle's). **Garrold Eft's body and journal** wait in the collapsed side passage with the phase's thesis: *"The Aerdy did not make the descent. The Aerdy permitted the descent. They were not the first. They are not the last."* And the **Permission Door** itself: no glow, no soul-test — **token, phrase, tally, witness.** Six methods plus the seventh the campaign has carried since Phase 4: **Elle's iron key**, her order's relic since the 374 CY expedition, unscrews into a working descent token — she is the seventh to carry it, and the first to know what it opens. Above the slab: **"Accounts above. Witness below."** Below it, the Stair Gallery, where surface sound stops arriving and Alicia's pact-mark goes altogether silent, down to the final lintel: *"It is not for those who arrive. It is for those who come back."*

### Phase Nine — Below the Clean Paper (Witness Station Seven)

The basement of every ledger the party has read. In the Account Hall, the slots are for **declarations, not keys** — *what you put in the slot is what you are* — and bay three holds the line the whole campaign has walked toward: **"H.V.A. — Recovered cargo. Escort: K. Witness pending. Recoverable but unreliable."** *Settled, delayed, transferred* was never metaphor. The **Conversion Note** pairs Korre's signed surface directive with the route clerk's shorthand of it — the cleanest single proof that respectable institutions feed the route. The **Recovery Crate Tag** — *"Claim precedes loss: yes. Surface panic acceptable: yes."* — closes the loop opened in Tamsin's counting house seven phases earlier: the claims Doril Veth wept over were priced down here first. The **Furnace Scrap** repeats the Itinerary's creed in a hand that belongs to no one in residence — your future ally seed. **Merrit Osk**, the unclaimed witness, is the phase's conscience. Above the Andren column and on the crate tag sits **the shallow arc with three notches** — E.'s under-road sigil; the party now knows her *mark* and still not her name. **Warden Savax** is not a villain but a career clerk ("If you are not witnessed, you are cargo"), and his standing instruction 6 is the punchline of the entire structure: questions of higher authority go through the channel beyond the **Black Ledger Gate**; the route's deeper figures answer *formally filed queries* only. **You cannot knock on Eclavdra's door. You have to file.** The gate — *"Unwitnessed cargo waits. Witnessed passage continues."* — checks category, not identity; beyond it, the **Lower Route Fork** offers three corridors (recovery, escort, supply), and retreat-with-proof is a fully honored ending. There is no boss fight. There is paperwork.

## The Descent Chain, Distilled

What actually takes them to the Underdark is not one clue but a convergence that closes every other direction:

1. **The relay tube** (P2) — information moves by a channel that is not post, sea, or road.
2. **The E. countersigns** (P2, Gilst's book) — the channel has an author.
3. **Veska's fourth hand** (P3) — the author is above everyone nameable.
4. **The registry annotation and Sereth's confession** (P4) — the author sets the calendar and is "not from this side of the great desert."
5. **Aldea's three findings** (P5) — the author is physically unreachable from any port and learned the surface hand from documents that traveled *upward*. The first arrow pointing down.
6. **The courier artery** (P5–6) — Pell → Quill → Kestrel & Reed → Korre → Kethren: the human ladder tops out at a boy whose gait is wrong.
7. **The Ashen Itinerary and the route mark** (P6) — the next leg bypasses surface travel *by definition*, and the term is older than the city.
8. **The triangulation** (P7) — five sources turn the phrase into a place: Tarnsmere, the Seven-Cut Cairn, a sealed stairwell.
9. **The Permission Door** (P8) — the descent is *administered*; the party crosses by satisfying an ancient procedure, or by Elle's key.
10. **Witness Station Seven** (P9) — the surface conspiracy resolves into a *client* of something older, and the Fork points deeper.

And the negative space matters as much: the roadhouse "R." goes nowhere by design, and the hunt for E. through people always ceilings out. Down is the only direction the evidence never exhausts.
