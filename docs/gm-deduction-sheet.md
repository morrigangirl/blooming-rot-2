# Blooming Rot 2 — The Deduction Sheet

*GM only. A table tool for right now: everything the party has enough evidence to have deduced by this point in Phase 2, each with the proof behind it and a line for handing it to them if they haven't said it themselves. Then the two pointers you asked for — Gilst's landlord, and the blighted ground against Kitty's tendrils — and the push that carries them out of Phase 2 and east.*

*A deduction "lands" when a player says some version of it out loud. Don't require the exact words. If a session ends and one of the starred items still hasn't landed, use its hand-it-to-them line — that's what Caelith and Trina are for.*

---

## What They Should Have Deduced By Now

**1. ★ Haskur's survival was engineered and paid for in advance — a service, not a miracle.** The contingency was cast *through* the focus; he has died at least twice before and been recovered each time; and the letter of credit was drawn six weeks before the fight, marked *"Hold for V., or his agent of record."* Somebody knew his timeline and had already paid for the pickup. *Hand it to them — Caelith:* "A man does not buy that kind of dying with his own purse. Someone keeps him the way a firm keeps a wagon."

**2. ★ There is a paymaster above Haskur, and Haskur is afraid of them.** One correspondent, three appearances, no face: the letters in Kael'thorne's cottage signed E., the wax sigil on the focus that is not in Haskur's hand, and the spellbook threat — *"You should fear your master more than your enemy… Fail me again and suffer the consequences. — E."* The man they killed was an employee, and a disappointing one. *Hand it to them — Trina:* "Read the threat again. That is not a rival's letter. That is a performance review."

**3. ★ It is an organization with bookkeeping, and people are the entries.** The ledger fragment — *VANDRELL settled, MORAVEN delayed, GILST transferred* — is three clerks' hands with no sums and no dates. It tracks people the way a bank tracks accounts, and two of the three names have since turned up as a dead clerk and a vanished grain factor: they are all on the same books. *Hand it to them — Caelith:* "Real settlement work carries figures. This carries names. People were the point."

**4. ★ The money is institutional, and it has a name: Cindren & Vhal.** The crane-and-coins is on Haskur's ring, his sealed letter, the warning envelope, and Gilst's registry card; the Charter Roll resolved it to a Rel Astran banking house with a Hardby office and a one-clerk satellite on Wick Lane. Guilds and vendettas don't underwrite a decade of resurrections. Banks do. *Hand it to them — Caelith:* "Respectable enough for clean rooms. Useful enough for dirty ones."

**5. ★ The scheme is grain fraud built on foreknowledge — being paid to know what tomorrow looks like.** Tamsin's last open ledger shows C&V Hardby's insurance claims filed *before* the losses occurred — by eleven days, three weeks, two days — with hundreds of thousands of gold riding on them; the seed pouch is sterile, washed show-grain, "proof" that can never be planted; and the counting-house carbons were stolen the day before anyone official came asking. *Hand it to them — Trina:* "Storms don't send word ahead. Clerks do."

**6. ★ The Yeomanry's own apparatus is compromised — not corrupted wholesale, but reachable.** The false letter of passage is an inside job: real vellum, real retired Bureau chief's hand, from an office that doesn't field traveling assessors. Gilst's death was certified by a contract physician who isn't on the coronial rota, and his posting was closed with no transfer and no questions. And Sara Thimblecut was poisoned *inside the gaol*, mid-sentence, within the hour of leaning toward a name. The machine can reach into bureaus, registries, and cells. *Hand it to them — Caelith:* "No one broke into the Yeomanry. Someone is using it exactly as designed, with the wrong hands on the levers."

**7. ★ The Hand's own office is infiltrated — nothing entrusted to the palace is safe.** Caelith said it plainly at the commission: his correspondence has been giving answers to questions that were never asked, and he will not pursue this through his office because he is not certain it is closed at every door. The party already acted on this once, rightly, when they abandoned the plan to petition the palace for gaol access. Keep rewarding that instinct. *Hand it to them — Caelith:* "Assume anything written to me through the palace is read twice. Write to me, or say it in this room."

**8. They are being watched in Loftwick, in real time.** The crane-and-coins envelope found them within a day of their return; Thimblecut was silenced within the hour of Elle's visit being arranged; the carbons vanished the day before Selvara reached the counting house. Somebody local is reporting their movements fast enough to act ahead of them. *(This one matures when they meet the watcher on Wick Lane — see the pointers below. If a player has already said "someone is watching us," they've got it.)*

**9. The smear against the Duke is manufactured — and inverted.** The leaflets accuse him of profiting from the grain scandal; in fact he stands secondary insurer on the fraudulent claims, so the scheme *costs* him. Whoever printed them knew the truth and reversed it, timed to hobble the one office likely to investigate. *Hand it to them — Trina:* "When a lie is that precise, it isn't gossip. Gossip gets details wrong. This got them backwards on purpose."

**10. The paymaster's culture is foreign in a way no one can place.** The script apes Elvish without being Elvish — Lorien, who has shelved everything, calls it "an attempted Elvish code"; the sigil is a wrong-shaped Elvish E; the lacquered tube's craft is courier-work of curious make with a seal that makes the wrong reader forget. Every expert says the same unhelpful thing: *elven, but peculiar.* That is as far as this deduction is allowed to go — the wall itself is the clue, though they won't know that for a long time. *(Never let a roll improve on "peculiar.")*

**11. The road runs east, and the timetable isn't local.** The map tube holds sea, trade, and caravan routes from Hardby; the credit was countersigned in Rel Astra; "Voren" lodged one night six weeks ago in the season the credit was drawn; Gilst's money came from "H.V. Hardby"; and the vellum strip — HARBOR BEFORE THRONE — is old imperial timing language about an eastern port. Loftwick is an outpost of this thing, not its home. *Hand it to them — Caelith, pinning the coast map:* "Everything you have found was paid for from the east. At some point you will have to go where the paying is done."

**12. The conspiracy is older and stranger than one dead mercenary — it runs assets that aren't people.** The same E. who threatened Haskur wrote letters to Kael'thorne, a fey creature that wore a mayor's face for years. Whatever E. is, it corresponds with banks *and* with things — and it has been working this region far longer than the party has been on the case. *(This usually lands on its own the moment someone remembers the cottage letters. If not, let Kitty's moment below do it — see the second pointer.)*

Everything on this list is fair. Nothing on it names Drow, the Underdark, or anyone called Eclavdra — and nothing should. If the table assembles all twelve, they have exactly the picture Phase 2 wants them to carry east: *someone institutional, foreign, patient, and already inside our walls paid for all of this — and every thread runs through Hardby.*

## Pointer One — Gilst's Landlord

The single most important unvisited room in Loftwick is two rooms above a stationer's on Wick Lane, and the way in is **Doman Reith** — Gilst's landlord, sixties, anxious, honest, and *waiting for someone safe to tell.* They're already standing on his street. Open the next session with him finding them:

*An older man is sweeping the stationer's doorstep for the third time in an hour, which is two more times than any doorstep needs. When he's sure no one else is in earshot: "You'll be from the palace, then? About Master Gilst?" He glances up at the shuttered windows above the shop. "You'd better come up before someone else does. There was a man here yesterday — watch tabard, spent a full hour in the rooms. Only the ribbon at his cuff was the wrong color, and I've been sweeping this step under real watchmen for thirty years."*

That wrong ribbon does three jobs at once: it gets them upstairs, it proves someone searched before them (and missed everything that matters), and it primes deduction 8 — they are not the only ones working this street. Inside, the searcher's leavings are the phase's treasure: the hollowed *Constitutional Commentaries* with the receipts book (four years of "H.V. Hardby," three entries countersigned **E.**), the throat-lozenge tin with the false bottom and the **bearer instrument on the C&V Hardby chop — the same hand as Vandrell's escrow**, the locket, the burned "Do not copy eastern marks twice," and the fear note behind the portrait. Read the fear note aloud; it is the room's heart. Honor Reith's three asks — take nothing without his knowing, keep his name away from the Watch, reseal the door with their own wax — and he stays what he is, the first ordinary person in Loftwick who chose to trust them.

And if Thimblecut wakes in the infirmary while they're working this street, her fact lands here with perfect timing: *the name she pointed at was Gilst, and Gilst didn't stay in that coffin.* The rooms above the stationer's suddenly matter twice.

## Pointer Two — The Blight, and Kitty's Tendrils

You wanted the blighted ground to react with Kitty's tendrils. Here's a way to stage it that pays now and keeps paying for six more phases — because it slots exactly into her recognition thread (the same ladder that later warms her veining at the E.-wax in Rel Astra and warms the seventh cut under her hand at the door).

**Bring the blight to her first.** Millicent's promised papers are already a dangling thread — "first thing tomorrow morning." Have them arrive on schedule, and with them a small crate of **sealed specimen jars from the Grain Register: blighted heads of durum from the struck districts,** kept as reference samples. The moment a jar is unstoppered near Kitty:

*The tendrils move first — before she's decided anything, the way a compass needle doesn't decide. A low warmth, a lean, the way skin turns toward a fire in a cold room. The blighted grain is years dead and it is the most alive thing her body has noticed since the Keep. And underneath the warmth, one wordless certainty, arriving like recognition rather than thought: this was not weather. This was made. And whatever made it is old, and it is far below her, and it already knows the shape of what she is.*

No check, no save — it's a gift, not a test. What it tells her is a *feeling with no vocabulary*: made-ness, depth, age, recognition. It never names anything, and no spell or roll sharpens it into words — that's the standing guardrail doing its job, and it makes the moment scarier, not less.

**Then give the reaction a use, because that's what makes it a clue instead of a mood.** Let Kitty try the seed pouch from the evidence table — and it gives her *nothing.* Cold, inert, dead as gravel. The show-grain is washed fake; the specimen jars are the real working. Her tendrils have just become the party's forgery detector for the entire grain fraud: real blight sings, counterfeit proof stays silent. That's deduction 5 confirmed by a completely independent sense — and it will matter again in Hardby, where "proof of origin" grain is the whole scam.

**And if they ride out to the fields, reward the trip.** A half-day east, toward the quiet farms out past Pleasant Hill, let them stand at a struck field's edge and see the thing that no natural blight does: **the rot ends at the boundary stone.** One farm's rows withered to the survey line; the neighbor's untouched, weed for weed the same ground. Kitty's tendrils hum louder here than at any jar. Gianni will see it too, in her own language — blights follow water and wind, and this one followed *paperwork.* Grain destroyed on a schedule, inside lines drawn on a deed. Nobody has to say the word "engineered." The field says it.

One caution: keep the tendrils tuned to *residue of the working* — blight, E.-wax, and much later the deep marks — never to people. She's a detector of craft, not of allegiance, or she'd unravel the mystery structure the campaign depends on.

## The Road Out of Phase Two

The remaining beats, in the order they want to fire: **Gilst's rooms** (through Reith, above), with **Hesren Vesh** at the notary door and **Pollow's two days** on the same street — grant the two days; a live frightened witness beats a fresh body. Working that street flushes **Sparrow**, and her dropped fold hands them "east on the Sevenday cart" no matter how the chase ends. Once two of the three names are firm, **Moll** calls with his drafts and his sincere wall; refusing him triggers **Sarth's night** in the cellar — decoys, the alarm rune, and the two prizes ("Tarsh," and the forged Audit Hall seal Caelith goes quiet over). Then Caelith's conclusion, the **three folios**, and the folded paper at the gate: *17 Cooper's Tale, Hardby.*

Before you put the folios on the table, run the gate in your head — five sentences the table should be able to say: *he survived by design; he's part of a network, not alone; the bank is the center and the live hand is in Hardby; Loftwick won't let us finish this here; we need to go east now, not eventually.* Deductions 1, 3, 4, 6, and 11 — if one is missing, its hand-it-to-them line above supplies it in Caelith's conclusion.

If they stall, escalate gently, in this order: Trina mentions, in passing, that Tamsin's daughter **Ailen is in Hardby, unprotected** — the human stake re-aims a drifting table faster than any clue. Then a visible clock tick with its telegraph (an item in the chest moved an inch overnight; the anonymous note; the broadsheet). Then Caelith pins the map: *"At Loftwick speed we lose Tamsin, and we lose Ailen. I would prefer not to."* Only after that, cut a thread they were dawdling on. And if the roadhouse "R." pulls them south first — let it. One session, a sister, a cedar box, no conspiracy at all. They'll come back knowing the difference between a mark and a door, and the Sevenday cart still leaves east on schedule.
