# Blooming Rot 2 — The Evidence Tie-Sheet

*GM only. This ties each of the twelve suppositions on the Deduction Sheet to the evidence your table actually put on the record, quoted from the Dead Man's Receipts session transcript with approximate line numbers so you can find the moments. Speakers: Aoibh (GM), Alannah (Gianni), Emma (Kitty), Gemma (Alicia), Katie (Selvara), Alyx (Elle). Quotes are lightly cleaned of speech-to-text noise; the numbering matches the Deduction Sheet exactly.*

*Where a supposition rests on something not yet said at your table, it's flagged as "Still missing" — those are your nudge targets.*

---

**1. Haskur's survival was engineered and paid for in advance.**
On the record: the session opened on his prior deaths — "Once in a bar fight" (GM, ~17) and Kitty's "an apparent drowning?" (~19). Kitty established the mechanism: "a very powerful spell had been channeled through the arcane focus. The nature of it was a contingency spell," with the matching pages "deliberately removed from the book after use" (~125–131). Kitty also read the letter of credit: "worth 10,000 gold pieces, specifically earmarked for ship, crew, supplies and travel… issued six weeks before the tower fight… included the note *hold for V, or his agent of record*" (~161–163). And at the Civic Register, the alias "Haskur Voren" lodged "about six weeks prior" — Kitty connected it on the spot: "Six weeks ago — that's…" the season of the credit (~2119–2129). Selvara closed the loop from the Keep itself: "They zapped themselves away, didn't they? Contingency" (~2565). **Fully landed.**

**2. There is a paymaster above Haskur, and Haskur is afraid of them.**
On the record: the spellbook threat was read aloud — "You should fear your master more than your enemy. This is your last chance… Fail me again and suffer the consequences," signed with "an initial at the end of it: E" (~1017–1019). Kitty immediately chained the instances: "Signed E — and there was an E on the focus, and the letter… was signed E as well" (~1033–1039), naming Mayor Thornwell's cottage as the source of the letters (~1035). The focus sigil itself: "an ancient and odd form of the Elvish letter E" (~145). And Gianni built the hold-over-him theory unprompted: whoever "is funding the contingency spell has ripped them out… It's my hold on you — you'll do what I say if you want these pages" (~905). **Fully landed — your table assembled "one correspondent, three appearances" themselves.**

**3. It is an organization with bookkeeping, and people are the entries.**
On the record: the fragment read aloud — "Vandrell settled. Moravan delayed. And Gilst, G-I-L-S-T, transferred" (~69). Then the table did the whole deduction live (~949–969): Alicia — "I was thinking of it as an outstanding debt… you're settled"; Kitty — "he's paid his debt. Somebody else has delayed their payment, and someone else — the debt's been bought out"; and Alicia found the key observation on her own: "it was described as a ledger… a ledger would normally have numbers, you'd think" (~965–967). That's the no-sums insight the design usually has to hand out. **Fully landed — and worth praising at the table; Alicia earned it.**

**4. The money is institutional, and it has a name: Cindren & Vhal.**
On the record: the signet ring — "the crane over three coins. Not a noble crest — belongs to a private banking house in the east… clearly worn regularly" (~155–157); the trade slug — "a Rel Astran accounting token used internally by banking houses" with "a private exchange mark" (~165); and the naming moment in the alcove: "The Heron and Three Coins… relates to the firm Cindren & Vhal" (rendered "Sendren & Vol" by the transcription, ~2111), with the satellite notary office and its "one clerk registered: Hesren, H-E-S-R-E-N, Vesh, V-E-S-H" (~2025–2027). Gilst's register card "is the same mark that you saw, with the heron and three" (~2109), and by day's end they'd stood in front of the placard on Wick Lane itself (~2591–2601). **Landed. Still missing:** the firm's *Hardby* office as the live operational hand — that proof is the bearer instrument waiting in the lozenge tin in Gilst's rooms.

**5. The scheme is grain fraud built on foreknowledge.**
On the record: the seed pouch — "sterile durum wheat and a hidden note: *Seed for the dry barony*. You identified the scam, Kitty — the grain is commonly used for false crop claims" (~183–185); Gianni then worked the mechanics aloud, twice: "if someone introduces sterile wheat into the supply chain then harvests could fail" (~807–819) and the thesis line worth framing: "Crop failures mean loans, loans mean debt, debt means control — and someone's using the Duke's own agricultural system to bleed them dry" (~871). At the counting house, Doril Veth showed the pattern in Tamsin's last open ledger: "insurance claims on three South Province grain shipments… filed before the losses supposedly happened" — the storm "eleven days before," the spoilage "three weeks before," the seizure "two days before" (~2719–2759) — with the carbon copies lifted from the book (~2795). **Fully landed, from two independent directions (the pouch and the ledger).**

**6. The Yeomanry's own apparatus is compromised — reachable, not corrupted wholesale.**
On the record: the false letter of passage "used genuine materials — real Yeomanry vellum, correct retired official signature," for a cover identity that fails on one fact: "crop estimation assessors don't travel. They are assigned to fixed locations" (~149–153); Alicia flagged "Voren" as an assumed name (~415). Gilst is "recorded dead" through the register with his posting simply closed (~2229, ~2035). And the gaol: Thimblecut looked at the list — "No, I… well, this one here" — and went down mid-sentence (~1330–1345), foxglove in quantity (~2305), inside a prison, within the hour. The party also learned the hard way that their tokens stop at institutional walls: "You've got a token of esteem… It'll get my attention, sure, but I can't take you anywhere. This is a prison" (~1401). **Landed. Still missing (a nuance, not a gap):** the certifying-physician detail — Reln Pollow, contract man off the coronial rota — is in your notes from the library scene but the name never landed cleanly in the transcript audio; re-say it when they reach his door on Wick Lane.**

**7. The Hand's own office is infiltrated.**
On the record, in Caelith's own commission speech: "I am entrusting you to this investigation because I cannot get my hands in it as the Hand of the Duke" (~499); "I am not entirely certain that my office is secure. I believe someone in my office has been leaking information to someone who is against the Duke" (~515); "I think someone in my office is utilizing the Duke's resources" (~529). Trina corroborated it sideways as gossip: Caelith is "driving her to distraction with his frustration over leaks within his office" (~2079). Note that at your table Caelith disclosed the Hand title at the commission itself — earlier than the module's script — so the party holds both halves of this already. **Fully landed.**

**8. They are being watched in Loftwick, in real time.**
On the record: Elle's insight at the gaol — "the timing of her illness, right when you're coming to speak to her, is awfully convenient… maybe somebody doesn't want her to talk" (~1381–1391), and her own summary later: "she started saying something about one of the names… and then started coughing and spluttering. I think she was poisoned!" (~2215). The counting-house carbons were lifted just ahead of them (~2795), and Doril's read — "she was searching, and this is the ledger that she left" (~2747) — put a second someone-got-here-first on the record the same day. **Landed on timing evidence. Still missing:** the *watcher herself* — that's Sparrow on Wick Lane, next session; and note the "We hope your visit to Loftwick is brief" envelope was a prior session, not this transcript, so a gentle recap line before the chase will re-arm it.

**9. The smear against the Duke is manufactured — and inverted.**
On the record: the street version first — "it's what's being reported in all of the leaflets. The Duke's been involved… a trade scandal that involved bad grain reports that didn't exist" (~1745–1749) — and then Doril Veth demolished it: "Cindren & Vhal are the ones who underwrote those contracts, and they're the ones taking money out of the Duke's pocket… the dukedom is the backstop, a secondary insurer… It's all a lie. And it was planted in that leaflet. There's no truth to it" (~2781–2789). Trina had already seeded the common-sense version: "the Duke is wealthy — why would the Duke bother with a grain conspiracy?" (~2081), and Caelith described himself "mired" in "faceless accusations" (~501–511). **Fully landed — Selvara's counting-house visit turned this from suspicion into arithmetic.**

**10. The paymaster's culture is foreign in a way no one can place.**
On the record: Kitty on the script — "kind of elfy, elven, elvish… similar, I think, to that sigil that was on the focus" (~107); the GM's identification — "an ancient and odd form of the Elvish letter E" (~145) and "an unusual linguistic style that appears elsewhere in the evidence" (~293); Alicia's identification of the tube's seal enchantment, the forget-discharge for wrong hands (~135); and Lorien's verdict at the library: "somebody's attempted an elvish code — more that, to me, than an actual language" (~2181). **Landed at exactly the depth it should be — "peculiar" — and it must stay there. No roll improves on Lorien.**

**11. The road runs east, and the timetable isn't local.**
On the record: the map tube — "a trade route, a shipping route and caravan routes from Hardby to Loftwick," with "a single R marked beside a roadhouse south of Loftwick" (~169–171); the Rel Astran token (~165); the credit "earmarked for ship, crew, supplies and travel" (~163); the cook — "Brennan Kepf said his niece's husband works the caravans to Hardby. He's heard the name Vandrell before — and the caravan departed yesterday" (~191); "Haskur Voren" lodging six weeks back in the credit's season (~2119); and Doril on Tamsin's trade — "moving Yeomanry winter wheat and durum to Aerdy coastal buyers at places like Hardby" (~2733). Even the vellum strip is on the record — immortalized by the transcription as "Harvard Fourth Throne" (~67), which I recommend never correcting at the table without smiling. **Landed as a direction. Still missing:** the *urgency* — that's what the Sparrow fold ("east on the Sevenday cart") and the bearer instrument's Hardby chop exist to supply.

**12. The conspiracy is older and stranger than one dead mercenary — it runs assets that aren't people.**
On the record: Kitty, connecting the cottage — "not the hag. She turned out to be Kael'thorne…" (~1047) — and the E-chain in the same breath (~1033–1039); Alicia taking "Kael Thorne" and the three names to the archives, and asking the question of the whole campaign out loud: "and who is E?" (~1081); and Kitty's archive result — Thornwell "doesn't turn up in them, despite the number of times that visited Loftwick" (~2133) — a mayor with no paper trail, which is its own kind of wrongness in a city that registers everyone (~1515). **Landed.** One table-canon jewel to keep: Emma's line at ~1047 calls Kael'thorne "the person who brought Kitty back." That's your own live thread — the creature that corresponded with E. has a prior claim on Kitty — and it makes the tendrils-and-blight pointer land even harder when you use it.
