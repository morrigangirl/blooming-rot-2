# GM Evidence Atlas — Where Everything Points

*Full-spoiler reference for the GM only. The complete evidence architecture of
Blooming Rot 2: what each piece establishes, what it points at, when it pays
off, and the chain that carries the party from a dead man's saddlebags to the
Underdark. Written against v1.4.8 canon (post-remediation), with the live
table's specific state folded in.*

---

## The truth, stated once

**"E." is Eclavdra of House Eilserv, drow, of Erelhei-Cinlu.** She is running
a decade-scale positioning operation, decades ahead of the events of *Vault of
the Drow*: cultivating surface commercial allies whose continued operation
provides cover, money and logistics for House Eilserv's much slower move. Her
instruments on the surface are, in descending order of knowledge: the
under-road courier network (Kethren's kind — hers), the handler layer (Korre,
Quill, Kestrel & Reed — paid, compartmentalized), the commercial layer
(Cindren & Vhal, embodied by Sereth — advised by annotation, never met), and
the political layer (the harbor-commission play — aimed at the Aerdy
succession). **Blooming Rot itself — the agricultural collapse — was an
engineered proof-of-concept:** grain destroyed on schedule, insurance claims
filed *before* the losses, panic measured and priced ("surface panic
acceptable: yes"), recovery rehearsed. The under-road the party finally walks
was not built by anyone living: it is an ancient descent **rented through the
paperwork of a dead Aerdy bureaucracy** (the Permissive Survey, Class VII),
and the horror of the campaign's second half is that the paperwork still
works.

The campaign's hinge, delivered in Caelith's Letter 6 and meant to be quoted
back at you: **"The conspiracy you have named is not the conspiracy."**

---

## The three braids

Every piece of evidence in nine phases belongs to one of three braids, and it
helps to know which one you are holding at any moment:

**Braid one — the money** (who paid): letter of credit → C&V marks → Hardby
chop → second set of books → Sereth's endorsement hand → harbor-commission
candidates → "the firm answers to the hand." This braid has a ceiling: it
ends at Sereth, who has never met his principal. It is designed to be
*completable* by Phase 4 and to feel, on completion, insufficient.

**Braid two — the people** (who knew): Gilst's fear note → Tamsin's queries →
Pollow's nerves → Hesren's "reads like Hardby work" → Vesh's/Mirelth's/
Tenrel's three modes of capture → Pell and Sera → Merrit Osk. This braid
carries the campaign's conscience. It also carries its most reliable
investigative engine: every witness knows exactly one layer up, and every
witness ends with some version of *"below," "east,"* or *"I never met him."*

**Braid three — the road** (how it moves): the lacquered relay tube → the
courier side-mark → Kestrel & Reed's box 17 → the eleventh-bell transfer →
"No sail. No road. VII Stair." → Tarnsmere → the Permission Door → the
Black Ledger Gate. **This is the braid that goes to the Underdark.** It is
the least visible early and the only one that never dead-ends.

## Phase-by-phase: what each piece is for

### Phase 1 — the strongroom seven (night showing)

The seven items do three jobs: establish foreknowledge, establish scale, and
plant two long fuses. **The letter of credit** is the spine — dated six weeks
before the fight, "Hold for V., or his agent of record": someone knew his
timeline, and V. has an *agent of record* (institutional language; the party
should feel the grammar of banking close around a resurrection). **The
crane-and-coins** (ring + sealed letter) is deliberately unnamed in Phase 1 —
the name Cindren & Vhal is the Records Alcove's payoff, so the mark stays a
shape until paper gives it a name. **The map tube's "R."** is the campaign's
one honest red herring: it leads to the Sign of the Brown Hare, Rosalin
Brent, and a cedar box of a dead man's childhood — no conspiracy content at
all, priced at five days and two clock segments, paying out only humanity
(and the never-sent letter that lands again in Phase 6). **The trade slug**
teaches Rel Astra's private economy exists. **The ritual focus** is a fuse:
it looks like Haskur's tool at night; daylight shows the wax sigil is *not
in his hand* — the first proof someone else laid the contingency.

### Phase 2 — daylight, receipts, and the first ceiling

The daylight re-examination converts objects into names: **the clipped
coin's countermark** resolves to C&V's internal clearing-mark; **the false
letter of passage** proves an *inside job* (real vellum, a retired Bureau
chief's real hand — institutions are already penetrated, which pre-loads the
Pell reveal three phases early); **the ledger fragment** — *settled, delayed,
transferred* — is the thesis of the whole campaign in six words: **people
carried as accounts.** Say those verbs again in Phase 9 when Haskur appears
in the Account Hall as "recovered cargo."

**The lacquered tube is braid three's opening move.** Courier-grade,
deniable, seal that makes you *forget*, interior wiped, heat-reactive
residue: a **relay**, not a container. Its lacquer-work and glyph are "elven
but peculiar" — the first material E.-artifact — and the hard rule attaches
here: *no roll, no spell, no sage names Drow.* Legend lore returns a verse
about a people who walked downward and were forgotten.

**Gilst's rooms are the load-bearing floor of the phase.** The receipts book
(41 entries, "H.V. Hardby") gives the money braid its ledger; the **three
entries countersigned "E."** open the formal E.-observation series; the
**bearer instrument** in the lozenge tin is the single most important paper
in the first act — same Hardby chop, same hand as Vandrell's escrow,
bridging Loftwick's dead clerk to Hardby's living operation. The **fear
note** ("the third winter I have been afraid") is braid two's charter.

The pressure apparatus — **Moll's bribe** and **Sarth's raid** — exists to
prove coordination between polite and criminal pressure. Sarth's forged
Audit Hall writ carries the stamp Caelith has hunted for nine months
(→ Ostren Pell, Phase 5); her broker's cutout name "Tarsh" (→ Anver Resh,
Phase 3). Hesren Vesh is the phase's moral tuning fork: a junior clerk who
knows nothing, whose courtesy conceals nothing, and whose courier message is
nevertheless what summons the knife. **Visiting the firm is what makes the
firm dangerous** — the party's first lesson that inquiry itself has a price.

**Your table's graft.** Your campaign gave E. an earlier, louder entrance
than the module's: the wax sigil on the focus, the threat scrawled on a
surviving spellbook stub — *"You should fear your master more than your
enemy… Fail me again and suffer the consequences. — E."* — and, before any
of it, **letters signed E. in Kael'thorne's cottage.** Treat the cottage
letters as observations zero: they establish that E. runs assets that are
not banks and not humans (a fey creature wearing a mayor), which quietly
*strengthens* the Letter-6 hinge — the conspiracy they can name (a bank!)
was never going to be the whole of a thing that also corresponds with fey.
Two rulings to hold: the script rules apply to the cottage letters
retroactively (elvish-but-not, no language check succeeds); and **the
partial fingerprint in the focus wax needs a policy.** Recommendation:
either it never matches anything (E. does not press seals herself — the
print belongs to a courier long since "settled"), or, if you want a payoff,
let it match **Kethren's kind** in Phase 8–9 (a route courier delivered the
focus) — never E. herself. Do not let a fingerprint shortcut a
five-phase reveal.

### Phase 3 — Hardby: the machine gets faces

**Veska Maelan's reading is the campaign's second act in one scene.** She
converts the party's objects into four names: **Solen Mereth** (chopped the
escrow; keeps the officially-destroyed defect-stamp; runs the second set —
the operational lever), **Tarlith Vhal Sereth** (the marginal endorsement
hand — the first name a court could use), **Anver Resh** ("Tarsh" — the
broker who hired Sarth), and **a fourth hand she cannot place** — E.
observations one through three (the loop, the serif, the angle of the
descender). The **defect-stamp** is physical perjury you can hold: certified
destroyed in 579, still stamping paper. The **claims-before-loss pattern**
(storm 11 days early, spoilage 3 weeks, seizure 2 days) is braid one's
smoking engine — *being paid to know what tomorrow looks like* — and your
table already owns it, having pulled it from Doril Veth's ledger in their
first Loftwick day.

The **Gynarchy registry** turns the vellum strip operational: three harbor
seats — First, Third, Fifth — emptied on schedule (Olm natural 580; Mirin
Vesh's staircase 581, Worth's work; Dale's brief illness 582, the
procedural-kill template), none refilled, because **whoever seats three
commissioners before the next Overking is named owns the eastern revenue.**
"Harbor before throne" stops being a motto and becomes a calendar. The
Tamsin thread resolves by travel approach (rescue at Black Cog, rescue at
Whitemoor, or the letter that will make your table go quiet), and the C&V
branch yields the indictment package by burglary, sympathy (Vella Tannin),
or subpoena. Note the ceilings again: Resh trades everything *except* his
Rel Astra contact; Sereth's name is reachable, Sereth's principal is not.

### Phase 4 — Rel Astra: completing the nameable conspiracy

The Three Seats teach the machine's three modes of capture: **Mirelth is
owned** (20,000 gp of C&V paper; §187 makes documentation itself the
weapon), **Vesh is leveraged** (Lyla's case — remove the pressure and the
honest man walks), **Tenrel is willing** (nothing to remove; only the
seating itself can stop her). Run all three and the party learns to read
capture anywhere for the rest of the campaign.

Two E. observations complete the surface set. **Observation four**, at the
imperial registry: the Third Seat's succession-deferral, filed days after
Mirin Vesh's inquest, annotated in the unplaceable hand — *"Sufficient.
Hold open until the second autumn after the next."* E. does not just fund
the conspiracy; **E. sets its calendar,** in writing, years ahead.
**Observation five**, from Sereth's own mouth on Path 2: *"The annotations
are not from this side of the great desert… The notes were always correct.
I do not ask further."* The named villain of the first act confesses that
he, too, reports upward into a void. Caelith's Letter 6 then does the
naming-without-naming: a hand that has not spent its life under Aerdi or
Yeomanry sun. **Five observations, no face — by design, the party leaves
their triumph with a question.**

### Phase 5 — Loftwick: the leak, and the first proof of "below"

The counterintelligence phase converts the oldest fuse: the Audit Hall seal
→ **Ostren Pell**, coerced through his sister Sera, handled through a
dead-drop → **Trell** → **Vesten Quill** → a Hardby-aligned letter agency at
a **Greyhawk address** (Kestrel & Reed). This is braid three surfacing as
an artery the party can walk *up*, packet by packet — and the five-choice
Pell decision (expose, turn, feed, shadow, remove) is the campaign's purest
ethics engine, with the unmentioned sixth choice priced accordingly.

**Aldea Veren's reply is the descent thread's first hard proof.** Three
findings, each fatal to a surface explanation: the wax matches **no surface
recipe**; the hand was **court-trained but not court-raised** — taught from
documents that *came up to the writer*, wrong stroke-order, a student who
never sat in the room; and the **geographic impossibility** — the earliest
dated annotation could not have been written by anyone reachable from any
sailing port in the window. Aldea will not write her speculation; asked the
right narrow question in person — *"could the writer have learned this hand
from someone who has never been to the surface?"* — she gives the party the
suspicion they will carry in their heads, unconfirmed, until Phase 8–9.

### Phase 6 — Greyhawk: the door

Six nodes, and the smallest mark in the phase is the most important one in
the campaign: beside "H. V. Andren" in the Brass Crow registry, **a vertical
stroke crossed at three-quarter height** — the foreign-correspondent
side-mark. It is **Kethren's courier mark**, and it will reappear on every
registry fragment below Tarnsmere. When a player recognizes it at the
Permission Door, the campaign clicks shut like a well-made box.

The nodes triangulate to the **eleventh-bell transfer in the Old
Stonecistern**: Haskur — alive, diminished, *being moved* — handed from
Korre's surface network to Kethren's route. What survives any outcome of
that night: the **route mark** (seven descending cuts beneath a black hill,
fresh soot), the **Ashen Itinerary** — *"No sail. No road. VII Stair."* and
*"Blooming was proof. Rot was proof. Recovery was the question."* — and
Iren Velash's keystone reading: **"This is not a sea-route term. It is a
descent term… older than the city. You will not find it on a port chart."**
Haskur's own line, if taken: *"I was not rescued. I was collected."* The
villain of the wilderness is cargo. Korre is the person the conspiracy is
willing to lose; Kethren is the person it is not; and the ladder of
ignorance reaches its top rung: *"Whoever pays them is not in the city."*

### Phase 7 — turning a phrase into a place

Five interpretive sources, five categories (name, function, permission,
material, place), no single point of failure: **Iren** (VII = a
route-class of *permitted descents* in obsolete Aerdy mining law; "no sail,
no road" is a literal transit condition), **Aldea** (mine-seal wax family;
iron-grey stone with a green thread — southern Cairn Hills; the "hill" is a
surveyor's convention for a **sealed entrance**), **Vone** (route-exception
ledger: the VII account prefix on Korre's traffic, destination *Tarnsmere
Toll*, his own note "T·S·C → Tarnsmere Spur Cairn → Guild sheet 47 →
confirm with surveyor" — the dead archivist's breadcrumb that proves the
party is on the path everyone else died on), **the Guild + Dalmor Vetch**
(claim VII/0341, claimant scraped, Survey seal re-waxed *seven years after
the Survey was dissolved*, Garrold Eft gone since 542; "miners call it a
stair when the earth charges first"; the seven cuts are **a permission
mark, not a count**), and **any prisoner** (Kethren: "Seven cuts. Beneath a
hill that is not a hill. Northeast."). The **False Stair** — six cuts, one
oak, paper too clean — is the conspiracy's own misdirection, and even
swallowed whole it proves the real marker by exclusion. The phase ends at
the **Seven-Cut Cairn**: the same mark the cistern wall wore, a hundred
miles north, in old soot — hand the rubbing across the table and let the
click happen.

### Phase 8 — the Permission Door

Tarnsmere Toll reverses every assumption: the toll window faces *downhill*
— **the tollhouse watched for people coming back up.** Seventeen tally cuts
behind the coat-rack, the bottom three recent. The registry fragment: *"No
sail. No road. Toll paid beneath."* — admissions with **"Return: nil"**
entries, two of them halfling cousins-of-record (Cam's payoff, now Elle's).
**Garrold Eft's journal** is the phase's conscience and its thesis: *"The
site is not a tollhouse. It is a witness-post… The Aerdy did not make the
descent. The Aerdy permitted the descent. They were not the first. They are
not the last."*

The **Permission Door** is the campaign's argument made architecture: no
glow, no soul-test — **token, phrase, tally, witness.** Paperwork, and the
paperwork works. Six methods plus the seventh the campaign has carried
since Phase 4: **Elle's iron key**, her order's relic since 374 CY,
unscrews to a working descent token — the sixth carrier never knew what it
opened. Above the slab: **"Accounts above. Witness below."** Below it, the
stair gallery where surface sound stops arriving, Alicia's pact-mark goes
silent, and the final lintel reads: *"It is not for those who arrive. It is
for those who come back."*

### Phase 9 — below the clean paper

Witness Station Seven is the basement of every ledger the party has read.
The Account Hall's slots are for **declarations, not keys** — *what you put
in the slot is what you are.* Bay three holds the line the whole campaign
has been walking toward: **"H.V.A. — Recovered cargo. Escort: K. Witness
pending. Recoverable but unreliable."** *Settled, delayed, transferred* was
never metaphor. The **Conversion Note** (Korre's signed surface directive
beside the route clerk's shorthand of it) is the cleanest single proof in
the campaign that respectable surface institutions feed the route. The
**Recovery Crate Tag** — *"Claim precedes loss: yes. Surface panic
acceptable: yes."* — closes a loop opened in a Loftwick counting house in
Phase 2: the claims Doril Veth wept over were priced down here first. The
**Furnace Scrap** repeats the Itinerary's creed in a hand that belongs to
no one in residence — your Phase 10 ally seed. Above the Andren column and
on the crate tag, **the shallow arc with three notches**: E.'s under-road
sigil — the party now knows her *mark*, still not her name. And Warden
Instruction 6 is the quiet punchline of the entire structure: *"Defer all
questions of higher authority to channel beyond Black Ledger Gate. The
route's deeper figures respond to formally filed queries; do not pursue
informally."* **You cannot knock on E.'s door. You have to file.**

The **Black Ledger Gate** — *"Unwitnessed cargo waits. Witnessed passage
continues."* — checks category, not identity; the **Lower Route Fork**
(recovery / escort / supply) is where Phase 10 will begin. There is no boss
fight. There is paperwork.

## The descent chain, distilled

What *actually* takes them to the Underdark is not one clue but a
convergence that closes every other direction:

1. **The relay tube** (P2) — foreign make, forget-seal: information moves by
   a channel that isn't post, sea, or road.
2. **The E. countersigns** (P2, Gilst's book) — the channel has an author.
3. **Veska's fourth hand** (P3) — the author is above everyone nameable.
4. **The registry annotation + Sereth's confession** (P4) — the author sets
   the calendar and is "not from this side of the great desert."
5. **Aldea's three findings** (P5) — the author is *physically unreachable
   from any port*, writes in unknown wax, and learned the surface hand from
   documents that traveled upward. First arrow pointing **down** rather
   than east.
6. **The courier artery** (P5–6) — Pell → Quill → Kestrel & Reed → Korre →
   Kethren: walked upward, the human ladder tops out at a boy whose gait is
   wrong and a transfer at the eleventh bell.
7. **The Ashen Itinerary + route mark + Iren's glossary** (P6) — "No sail.
   No road. VII Stair": the next leg *bypasses surface travel by
   definition*, and the term is a descent term older than the city.
8. **The triangulation** (P7) — five sources turn the phrase into a
   place: Tarnsmere Toll, the Seven-Cut Cairn, a sealed stairwell.
9. **The Permission Door** (P8) — the descent is *administered*; the party
   crosses not by force but by satisfying an ancient procedure (or by
   Elle's key — the campaign's oldest object turning out to be a ticket).
10. **Witness Station Seven** (P9) — the surface conspiracy resolves into a
    *client* of something older; the Fork offers three directions deeper.

And the negative space matters as much: **the roadhouse "R." goes nowhere**
(by design — it teaches the table that not every mark is a door, and pays
its way in grief instead of intel), and **the hunt for E. through people
always ceilings out** — Sarth won't name her broker, Resh won't name Rel
Astra, Sereth never met the writer, Korre never asked, Kethren says only
"the road is below." When every witness ends at the same wall, the wall is
the clue. Down is the only direction the evidence never exhausts.

## Watch-list for your table specifically

They currently hold: the full strongroom/daylight set, three E. artifacts
(cottage letters, focus sigil, spellbook threat — observations "zero"),
Cindren & Vhal named, Hesren Vesh named, Gilst dead-on-paper with Pollow's
certification, Tamsin missing with claims-before-loss already discovered,
Thimblecut poisoned mid-name (your table-emergent thread — decide who
ordered it; the module's nearest analog is the Wick Lane watcher
apparatus), and the roadhouse R. exerting its gravitational pull. Standing
guardrails from here to Phase 7: never name Drow, Eclavdra, Eilserv, Vault,
or Underdark under any roll; keep every script reading at "elven but
peculiar"; the "three-rayed sun" wax school stays unnamed; Trina's patron
stays unnamed; do not let *legend lore*, the fingerprint, or a clever
Insight collapse the ladder. The reveal is not a fact the party finds — it
is a floor they eventually stand on.
